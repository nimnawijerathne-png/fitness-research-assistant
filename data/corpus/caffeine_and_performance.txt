Caffeine and Exercise Performance

Caffeine is one of the most well-researched and consistently effective legal performance-enhancing substances, with benefits shown across endurance exercise, high-intensity intermittent exercise, and to a lesser extent, strength/power activities.

A commonly cited effective dose range is 3-6 mg of caffeine per kilogram of body weight, taken approximately 30-60 minutes before exercise, though individual sensitivity varies considerably and some benefit has been observed even at lower doses.

Regular, habitual caffeine users may experience somewhat reduced performance benefits compared to infrequent users, due to tolerance, though research suggests meaningful performance benefits often persist even with regular use.

Caffeine's proposed performance mechanisms include reduced perceived exertion (exercise feels easier at a given intensity), central nervous system stimulation, and increased fat utilization as fuel, which may help spare limited glycogen stores during longer exercise.

Excessive caffeine intake can cause negative effects including increased heart rate, anxiety, and sleep disruption — the latter being particularly relevant since caffeine's stimulant effects can persist for many hours, potentially impairing the sleep needed for recovery if consumed too late in the day.
