Cardiovascular Exercise Guidelines

The World Health Organization recommends adults get at least 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75-150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity, per week, for substantial health benefits.

Moderate-intensity activity (e.g. brisk walking, easy cycling) allows a person to talk but not sing during the activity. Vigorous-intensity activity (e.g. running, fast cycling) makes talking in full sentences difficult.

Cardiovascular training improves heart and lung efficiency, lowers resting heart rate over time, and is associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and some cancers.

Two broad training approaches are commonly compared: steady-state cardio (sustained effort at a consistent moderate intensity) and high-intensity interval training (HIIT — short bursts of near-maximal effort alternated with rest or low-intensity recovery). Research generally shows HIIT can produce comparable or superior cardiovascular fitness improvements in less total time, but steady-state cardio tends to be easier to sustain for longer durations and lower-impact on joints.

Combining resistance training with cardiovascular exercise is generally recommended over doing either exclusively, since each provides distinct health benefits that don't fully overlap.
