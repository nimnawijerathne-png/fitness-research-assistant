Energy Balance and Weight Loss

Weight loss fundamentally requires a sustained caloric deficit — consuming fewer calories than the body expends. While the quality and source of calories affects satiety, adherence, and health markers, total energy balance remains the primary determinant of weight change.

A commonly cited moderate deficit is 500 calories per day below maintenance, which theoretically produces roughly 0.5 kg (about 1 lb) of weight loss per week, though individual results vary due to metabolic adaptation, water retention, and measurement inaccuracies.

Very aggressive caloric deficits are generally discouraged for sustained weight loss, as they are associated with greater muscle loss alongside fat loss, increased hunger and fatigue, and higher likelihood of abandoning the diet.

Resistance training during a caloric deficit helps preserve muscle mass that would otherwise be lost alongside fat, since the body is more likely to break down muscle tissue for energy when it isn't given a reason (via training stimulus) to keep it.

Weight loss plateaus are common and often result from metabolic adaptation (the body becoming more energy-efficient at a lower weight) combined with underestimated calorie intake or overestimated calorie expenditure — both frequently reported issues in self-tracked diet data.
