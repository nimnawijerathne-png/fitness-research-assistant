Hydration and Exercise Performance

Even mild dehydration (as little as 2% of body weight lost through fluid) can measurably impair endurance performance, cognitive function, and perceived exertion during exercise.

General hydration guidance suggests consuming fluids throughout the day rather than relying solely on thirst, since thirst is a lagging indicator that often appears after mild dehydration has already begun.

During exercise lasting under an hour, plain water is generally sufficient for most people. For exercise lasting longer than 60-90 minutes, especially in heat, electrolyte replacement (particularly sodium) becomes more important to prevent hyponatremia and maintain performance.

A simple practical check for hydration status is urine color — pale yellow generally indicates adequate hydration, while dark yellow suggests a need to increase fluid intake.

Overhydration is also a risk, particularly in endurance events, where excessive plain water intake without electrolyte replacement can dilute blood sodium levels (hyponatremia), which in severe cases is dangerous. This is why electrolyte balance, not just total fluid volume, matters for longer-duration exercise.
