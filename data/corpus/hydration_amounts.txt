Specific Hydration Amounts for Exercise

General sports science guidance suggests consuming approximately 400-600 ml (about 14-20 fl oz) of fluid in the 2-3 hours before exercise, allowing the body time to absorb it and excrete any excess before activity begins.

During exercise, a commonly cited guideline is 150-350 ml (about 5-12 fl oz) of fluid every 15-20 minutes, adjusted based on sweat rate, exercise intensity, and environmental temperature — higher heat and humidity increase fluid needs significantly above this baseline range.

A practical way to estimate individual sweat rate is to weigh oneself before and after a one-hour exercise session without consuming fluids: each kilogram (2.2 lb) of weight lost represents approximately 1 liter of fluid that should be replaced afterward, plus continued replacement of any ongoing losses.

For exercise exceeding 60-90 minutes, adding approximately 300-700 mg of sodium per liter of fluid consumed is commonly recommended to help maintain electrolyte balance and reduce hyponatremia risk, which plain water alone does not address.

After exercise, replacing roughly 1.25-1.5 liters of fluid for every kilogram of body weight lost during the session is a commonly cited target for full rehydration, since fluid losses continue via urine output even after activity ends.
