Injury Prevention in Exercise

A proper warm-up before exercise increases muscle temperature, blood flow, and joint range of motion, which is associated with reduced injury risk compared to exercising without one. Dynamic warm-ups (movement-based, e.g. leg swings, bodyweight squats) are generally recommended over static stretching before activity, as static stretching held before exercise has been associated with small, temporary reductions in muscular power output.

Static stretching (holding a stretch for 20-30+ seconds) is more commonly recommended after exercise, when it does not carry the same performance-reduction concern and may support flexibility gains over time.

Common causes of exercise-related injury include rapid increases in training volume or intensity ("too much, too soon"), poor exercise technique, inadequate recovery between sessions, and continuing to train through pain rather than addressing it early.

The "10% rule" is a commonly cited (though not universally agreed upon) guideline suggesting weekly training volume — such as running distance — should not increase by more than roughly 10% from one week to the next, to reduce overuse injury risk.

Pain that is sharp, localized, or worsens during activity is generally considered a signal to stop and assess, as opposed to general muscular fatigue or mild delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS), which is a normal response to unfamiliar or increased training stress and typically resolves within 2-3 days.
