Macronutrients Overview

The three macronutrients — protein, carbohydrate, and fat — each play distinct roles and provide different amounts of energy: protein and carbohydrate provide approximately 4 calories per gram, while fat provides approximately 9 calories per gram.

Carbohydrates are the body's primary and most readily accessible energy source, particularly for higher-intensity exercise. They are stored in muscles and the liver as glycogen, which becomes the dominant fuel source during moderate-to-vigorous activity.

Dietary fat supports hormone production (including testosterone, which plays a role in muscle growth), aids absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), and serves as a significant energy source during lower-intensity, longer-duration activity.

There is no single "correct" macronutrient ratio for everyone — appropriate distribution depends on individual goals (fat loss, muscle gain, endurance performance), activity level, and personal preference/adherence, though most general health guidelines suggest carbohydrates and fat should each make up a meaningful portion of daily calories alongside adequate protein.

Micronutrients (vitamins and minerals), while needed in much smaller quantities than macronutrients, remain essential — inadequate intake of nutrients such as iron, vitamin D, or B12 can impair energy levels and exercise performance even when macronutrient and calorie intake appear adequate.
