Menstrual Cycle Considerations in Training

The menstrual cycle involves fluctuating levels of estrogen and progesterone across roughly four weeks, which some research suggests may influence energy levels, perceived exertion, and recovery capacity differently across cycle phases, though individual variation is considerable.

Some research suggests the follicular phase (roughly the first half of the cycle, after menstruation and before ovulation) may be associated with slightly improved strength and power performance for some individuals, though findings across studies are mixed and not universally consistent.

Iron losses through menstruation can contribute to lower iron stores in some menstruating athletes, which is relevant to fitness since iron is essential for oxygen transport in the blood, and low iron levels are linked to reduced endurance performance and increased fatigue.

Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S) is a condition where inadequate caloric intake relative to exercise energy expenditure can disrupt hormonal function, including menstrual cycle regularity, and is associated with negative effects on bone health, immune function, and performance — irregular or absent periods in an athlete should not be assumed to simply be a normal consequence of training.

General individual tracking (noting energy, performance, and mood across cycle phases over a few months) is often suggested as a more useful approach than applying generic cycle-based training templates, given how much variation exists between individuals.
