Protein Intake for Muscle Growth and General Health

The general Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for protein is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day for sedentary adults. However, this figure is considered a minimum to prevent deficiency, not an optimal amount for people who exercise regularly.

For individuals engaged in regular resistance training, research commonly cited in sports nutrition literature suggests a range of 1.6-2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day supports muscle protein synthesis and recovery more effectively than the base RDA.

Protein needs are generally not meaningfully higher for endurance athletes compared to strength athletes, though endurance athletes do have elevated protein needs compared to sedentary individuals due to muscle repair demands from repeated training stress.

Distributing protein intake relatively evenly across 3-4 meals per day, rather than consuming it all in one meal, is often associated with better muscle protein synthesis response throughout the day, though total daily intake appears to matter more than precise timing for most people.

Complete protein sources (containing all essential amino acids) include meat, fish, eggs, dairy, and soy. Combining incomplete plant protein sources (e.g. rice and beans) across a day can also provide a complete amino acid profile for those following plant-based diets.
