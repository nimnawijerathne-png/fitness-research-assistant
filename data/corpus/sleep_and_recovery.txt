Sleep's Role in Fitness and Recovery

Sleep is when much of the body's physical recovery occurs, including the release of growth hormone, which supports muscle repair and tissue growth. Most adults require 7-9 hours of sleep per night for optimal physical and cognitive recovery.

Sleep deprivation has been associated with reduced muscle protein synthesis, impaired glucose metabolism, increased cortisol (a stress hormone that can promote muscle breakdown when chronically elevated), and reduced exercise performance and motivation.

Athletes and highly active individuals may benefit from sleep durations at the higher end of the recommended range (8-10 hours), as training itself increases the body's recovery demands compared to a sedentary lifestyle.

Sleep quality, not just duration, matters — frequent awakenings or reduced time in deep sleep stages can impair recovery even if total time in bed meets recommended durations.

Practical habits associated with better sleep quality include maintaining a consistent sleep and wake schedule, limiting screen exposure and bright light in the hour before bed, avoiding caffeine within roughly 6 hours of bedtime, and keeping the sleep environment cool and dark.
