Strength Training Fundamentals

Strength training involves working muscles against resistance to build size, strength, and endurance. The American College of Sports Medicine recommends adults perform resistance exercises for all major muscle groups at least two non-consecutive days per week.

Beginners typically see the fastest strength gains in the first 8-12 weeks, largely due to neuromuscular adaptation — the nervous system learning to recruit muscle fibers more efficiently — rather than muscle growth itself. Actual muscle hypertrophy becomes the dominant driver of strength gains after this initial period.

A basic beginner program typically includes compound movements (squats, deadlifts, presses, rows) that work multiple muscle groups at once, rather than isolation exercises that target a single muscle. Compound lifts are generally recommended first because they build a foundation of overall strength and movement patterns.

Rest between sets for strength-focused training (heavy weight, low reps) is typically 2-5 minutes to allow adequate recovery of the phosphagen energy system. Rest for hypertrophy-focused training (moderate weight, moderate reps) is typically 60-90 seconds.

Common beginner mistakes include increasing weight too quickly before mastering form, skipping warm-up sets, and training the same muscle group on consecutive days without adequate recovery.
