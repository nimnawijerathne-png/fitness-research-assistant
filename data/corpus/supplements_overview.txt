Common Fitness Supplements: An Overview

Creatine monohydrate is among the most researched fitness supplements, with strong evidence supporting improvements in high-intensity, short-duration strength and power performance, and is generally considered safe for long-term use in healthy individuals at standard doses (commonly 3-5 grams per day).

Whey and other protein supplements are a convenient way to help meet daily protein targets but provide no benefit beyond what equivalent protein from whole food sources would provide — they are a convenience tool, not a substance with unique physiological effects beyond their protein content.

Beta-alanine has research support for improving performance in high-intensity exercise lasting roughly 1-4 minutes, working through increased muscle carnosine levels, which helps buffer acid buildup during intense effort. A common harmless side effect is a tingling sensation (paresthesia).

Branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) have weaker supporting evidence for muscle growth specifically when total daily protein intake is already adequate, since whole protein sources already provide these amino acids alongside the full amino acid profile needed for muscle repair.

Supplement quality and regulation vary significantly by country; third-party testing certifications (such as those verifying a product is free of banned substances) are commonly recommended for competitive athletes to reduce the risk of inadvertent contamination.
